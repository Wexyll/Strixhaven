Strixhaven

A guide for players joining the campaign: the university, its five colleges, life on campus, and how to build the student you’ll play.

You’ve been accepted

Dear incoming student,

Congratulations. You have a place at Strixhaven, the most celebrated university of magic in the multiverse. Five elder dragons founded it so that mages could study the magic each of them had mastered, and those dragons still roam the world today. Our students come from everywhere: other nations, other continents, and more than a few other worlds.

Your first year is shared. Every first-year follows the same broad curriculum, wears the same grey-and-white uniform and lives on the central campus. At the start of your second year you will declare one of our five colleges. Until then, attend your lectures, sit your exams, and try not to set anything important on fire.

The Office of Admissions

What this campaign is

Strixhaven is a campaign about being a student as much as being an adventurer. Expect lectures, exams, parties, part-time jobs and a fierce sports rivalry alongside the mysteries and the fights. The friendships and rivalries your character builds with other students carry real mechanical weight, so the social side of the game matters here more than usual.

We start on the first day of term, with everyone at 1st level, and you’ll level up as the school year goes on.

How the colleges work

Each college grew out of one founder dragon’s magic, and each is run by two deans who teach opposite answers to the same question. Lorehold argues about order and chaos; Prismari about perfection and expression; and so on. Your character hasn’t declared yet, but knowing which dean they’d side with is a great shortcut to who they are.

Lorehold

College of Archaeomancy, founded by Velomachus Lorehold

History, brought back to life. Lorehold mages call up the spirits of the past to question them, excavate ruins and catacombs, and treat the dead as their best primary sources. Its curriculum overlaps with Prismari’s in art history.

Augusta Dean of Order

History has patterns. Learn its structure and you can predict, and shape, what comes next.

Plargg Dean of Chaos

History is a pile of accidents and personalities. You learn it by getting your hands dirty.

Suits archaeologists, tomb-delvers, mediums, keepers of family legends, and anyone who would happily argue with a ghost. Clerics, wizards and artificers fit naturally, as do paladins and a rogue who robs tombs “for research”.

Magic as art. Prismari students paint with fire, sculpt with ice and dance with lightning. Music, theatre, fashion and pure spectacle, all made out of raw elemental power, and all a little too loud.

Life on campus

Most of what makes Strixhaven different happens between adventures. These are the light rules we use to track student life, plus the clubs and jobs you can sign up for.

Student Dice

A Student Die is a d4 tied to one or two skills. When you make an ability check with one of those skills, you can roll it and add it, even after you’ve seen the d20, as long as I haven’t announced the result yet. You can add only one Student Die to a check, and each one comes back when you finish a long rest. You earn them from clubs and from doing well in exams.

Exams

  1. Study (optional)The night before, make a check to study. Succeed and you can reroll one exam check. Pull an all-nighter for a second reroll, but sit the exam at disadvantage.
  2. Sit the examEach exam is two ability checks, often one testing knowledge and one testing judgment or presence.
  3. Get your resultPass one check and you pass. Pass both and you ace it. Fail both and you lose your clubs to mandatory tutoring until you pass the next exam.

Relationships

Time spent with fellow students, in class, at work or in your clubs, earns Relationship Points with them. They can go up or down.

The college campuses
Each college has its own campus around the central one. You’ll spend time on all five in your first year.

Mage Tower

Strixhaven’s favorite sport. Two teams of five mages face off in the stadium, each with its college mascot perched on top of a tall tower. You score by stealing the other team’s mascot and carrying it back to your own tower. Any spell is legal unless it would hurt a player, a mascot or the crowd, in which case it simply fizzles. Try out for a team, join the cheer squad, or just pick a side and shout.

Jobs

A job pays 5 gp at the start of every week, and you’ll be scheduled alongside a fellow student. When you take the job, you gain a Relationship Point with that coworker, and you choose whether it’s positive or negative.

Clubs

You can join up to two clubs. Each one gives you a Student Die for its two skills and puts you in a room with its members every week. Pick a skill to see which clubs boost it.

    Making your student

    You build a Strixhaven character like any other D&D character, with one twist: your background is your college. Here are the steps, followed by the advice that players and DMs who’ve been through Strixhaven keep repeating.

    1. Start with a student, not a build

      Before you touch stats, answer three questions. Why did they enroll? What do they want to walk away with? And what would make them stay when the semester turns dangerous? A character with a reason to be at school, not just a reason to adventure, gets the most out of this campaign.

    2. Choose your intended college

      You won’t officially declare until second year, but your background shows where you’re leaning. Each college background gives you two skills and the Strixhaven Initiate feat: two of the college’s cantrips, plus one 1st-level spell you can cast once per long rest for free (or with your own spell slots). You pick Intelligence, Wisdom or Charisma as the casting ability. If you already cast spells, the college also adds its spells to your class list.

      The full details for each college are on the Welcome tab.

    3. Pick a species and class

      Strixhaven draws students from everywhere, so almost any species fits; ask me if you want something unusual. The setting’s own species is the owlin: owlfolk who can fly and move silently. Every class works here, including fighters, rogues, barbarians and monks. Thanks to the Initiate feat, everyone casts a little. From 4th level, the Strixhaven Mascot feat lets you bond with your college’s mascot as a familiar.

    4. Plan your student life

      Pick up to two clubs from the Campus life tab and decide whether you want a job. Clubs that cover skills you’re weak in will help in exams; clubs that suit your character will help in roleplay. Ideally, both.

    Advice from other Strixhaven tables

    • Martial characters hold up fine.

      The college backgrounds give every student some spellcasting, and people who’ve run the book point out they were built partly so non-casters keep pace at a school of magic. Half-casters like rangers and paladins have no trouble at all.

    • You don’t need a high Intelligence.

      Exams lean on Intelligence, but rarely only on it. They mix skills, studying gives you rerolls, and failing mostly just costs you your club dice for a while. Playing the student who scrapes by is a valid, and very fun, choice.

    • Choose your college for the story first.

      Each college is a question with two answers. Deciding which dean your character would side with gives you a personality in one sentence, and a built-in argument with half your classmates.

    • Know what each college does mechanically.

      Build guides broadly agree: Silverquill is excellent for Charisma casters, with strong social skills and silvery barbs. Witherbloom turns a wizard into a capable healer. Quandrix is packed with utility spells. Lorehold rewards Intelligence and Wisdom characters who like to know things. Prismari suits blasters who build around its elemental spells.

    • Lean into the social game.

      Relationships are a real mechanic here. A character who wants to make friends, or enemies, will get more boons, more scenes and more story than one who keeps to themselves.
