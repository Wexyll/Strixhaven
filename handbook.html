Before Session One

Building Your Student

You build a normal level-1 D&D character, then layer on the things that make you a Strixhaven mage. You do not have to play a spellcaster: the college options let any class dip a toe into magic.

Your College Background

Each of the five colleges has its own background (Lorehold, Prismari, Quandrix, Silverquill, Witherbloom). Like any background it gives you skill and tool proficiencies plus starting gear, but it also carries two special perks:

  • The Strixhaven Initiate feat, which grants you two cantrips and a 1st-level spell drawn from your college's theme (you can cast that spell for free once between rests).
  • A growing college spell list: as you gain levels, you add two college-flavored spells to your options each spellcasting level. This is how a Witherbloom fighter picks up a little healing, or a Prismari rogue learns to throw fire.

Non-spellcasters still get the proficiencies and the feat's cantrips, so no class is left out. Exact spell lists live in Chapter 2 of the book.

Species & Feats

All the usual D&D species are welcome, plus the setting's own: the owlin, owl-folk with darkvision, a flying speed, and a knack for stealth. Two Strixhaven feats are on the table too: Strixhaven Initiate (the cantrips-and-spell one your background grants) and Strixhaven Mascot, which lets you summon your college's mascot creature to lend a hand.

Player Tip

Pick the college that fits the character you want to play, not just the strongest spell list. You'll spend three years steeped in its culture, and the roleplay is the whole point.

The Daily Grind

Jobs, Clubs & Student Dice

College life is more than lectures. What you do outside class earns you a small but genuine mechanical edge.

Jobs & Extracurriculars

You can take a campus job (barista at Firejolt Cafe, reporter for the Strixhaven Star, library page, professor's assistant, mascot wrangler) and join clubs of every stripe, from dueling societies and choirs to dragonchess and, yes, the campus LARPers. Beyond the roleplay, each activity teaches your character something.

What You Earn: Student Dice Student Die = d4

Jobs, clubs, and exams reward you with student dice. A student die is a d4 you can add to an ability check when the skill lines up with what that activity taught you. Work the campus paper long enough and you'll have a nose for a story, so you might spend a die on an Insight check.

The catch: a student die is one-time use. Once you roll it, it's spent. Think of it as a bit of self-earned good luck you've banked for the right moment.

Player Tip

Don't overload. Chasing every club plus a job can eat into study time, and that comes back to bite you at exams. Pick a couple of things you'd actually care about and lean in.

The Part Everyone Dreads

Classes & Exams

You'll be enrolled in themed classes with names like "Ethics of Enchantment" or "Introduction to Reanimation," and periodically you'll be graded. There are only a handful of exams across a year (roughly three in your first), so each one matters.

How an Exam Works

An exam is a short skill challenge. You make a series of ability checks tied to two specific skills for that class. For every check you pass, you earn a student die you can later use on those same skills. Do well and you walk away with a handful of dice; bomb it and you leave empty-handed.

Study First. Always.

Before an exam you can study, which hands you re-rolls to shore up your odds on the test. There is essentially no downside, so study every time: it's the difference between scraping by and acing it. If you're truly desperate you can try to cheat, but if you're caught, the consequences are real (and expulsion is not off the table).

Good to Know

Your DM may run these systems in full, lightly, or not at all. Ask at session zero how much of the college-life machinery you'll actually be using, so you know where to spend your attention.

The Heart of It

Friends, Rivals & Beloveds

The People Make the Place

You'll cross paths with a whole cast of fellow students, in class, at your job, at club meetings. How you treat them shapes each into a Friend, a Rival, or a Beloved, and those bonds carry real weight.

A Friend or Beloved grants you Bond Boons: favors, backup, or an edge when you need one. A Rival brings Bond Banes: they needle you, undercut you, and generally make your life harder. Tending your relationships is one of the most reliable advantages at the whole school, and usually the most fun.

Putting It Together

How to Manage Your Year

Six habits that keep a Strixhaven student on top of it all.

  1. Study before every exam.

    It's free odds. There's no reason to skip it, and the student dice you earn snowball.

  2. Match your job and club to the checks you care about.

    Your student dice only help on related skills, so aim them where your character wants to shine.

  3. Spend student dice deliberately.

    They're one-time and only a d4, so hold them for a check that genuinely matters rather than burning them on trivia.

  4. Invest in a couple of Friends.

    Bond Boons are a real edge. And keep an eye on your Rivals so their Banes don't blindside you.

  5. Don't overcommit.

    Too many activities plus a job cuts into studying. Depth beats breadth here.

  6. Keep a simple tracker.

    Jot down your dice, your job, your club, and where each relationship stands. Your DM keeps a sheet too; you'll thank yourself later.
